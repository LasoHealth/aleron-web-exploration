Patient picture

A 47-year-old man whose record reads as one metabolic process expressed in five places rather than five independent findings. Insulin resistance, central adiposity, untreated stage 2 hypertension and low-grade inflammation move together, and the overnight oximetry adds a plausible driver that nothing in the record has yet tested for. Aerobic capacity is low and self-reported energy and cognitive clarity are both at 4 of 10, which is consistent with the same picture rather than with a separate mood or sleep complaint. The heritable finding, an ATM pathogenic variant, sits outside that process and is on its own track.

Cross-domain patterns (4)

SYS-P1

Insulin resistance driving both lipid particle burden and blood pressure

high trend

HOMA-IR 4.3 with fasting insulin 16 µIU/mL sits alongside triglycerides 180 mg/dL, HDL-C 40 mg/dL, ApoB 112 mg/dL and clinic BP 142/90 mmHg. Three of the five domain models are reading the same underlying state through different inputs, so their tiers are not independent and should not be added.

Mechanism
Hyperinsulinemia increases hepatic VLDL secretion and renal sodium reabsorption at the same time, which raises triglyceride-rich particle count and intravascular volume together. Sympathetic tone rises with both.
Signals
  • · HOMA-IR 4.3 (insulin-resistant, threshold 3.0)
  • · triglyceride/HDL-C ratio 4.5
  • · waist 105 cm with BMI 29.4
  • · BP 142/90 mmHg untreated, no ABPM on file
Data types labs vitals anthropometrics

SYS-P2

Untested sleep-disordered breathing as a shared upstream cause

medium snapshot manual review

Overnight SpO2 nadir 89 % with an overnight resting heart rate of 38 bpm, on 82 % wearable coverage. Nocturnal desaturation of this degree, in a man with a waist of 105 cm and untreated stage 2 hypertension, is the single cheapest explanation available for the hypertension, the insulin resistance and the reported energy and clarity scores at once. No sleep study is on file, so the pattern is asserted from wearable data alone.

Mechanism
Repeated intermittent hypoxia raises sympathetic outflow and cortisol, which impairs peripheral glucose disposal and sustains nocturnal pressure. The bradycardia is consistent with high vagal tone between events rather than with athletic conditioning at a VO2max of 28 mL/kg/min.
Signals
  • · SpO2 nadir 89 % (desaturation)
  • · overnight RHR 38 bpm against VO2max 28 mL/kg/min
  • · HRV 34 ms against a 42 ms baseline
  • · self-reported energy 4/10, cognitive clarity 4/10
Data types wearable patient-reported vitals

SYS-P3

Inflammation elevated out of proportion to the lipid panel

medium trend

hs-CRP 3.0 mg/L is high while LDL-C 132 mg/dL and Lp(a) 25 nmol/L are unremarkable. Adiposity accounts for part of it. The residual is unexplained, and infection has not been formally excluded in the record.

Mechanism
Visceral adipose tissue secretes IL-6, which drives hepatic CRP production independently of particle burden. Intermittent hypoxia adds to the same pathway, which is why this pattern and SYS-P2 are not independent.
Signals
  • · hs-CRP 3.0 mg/L (high)
  • · LDL-C 132 mg/dL, Lp(a) 25 nmol/L (both unremarkable)
  • · no second hs-CRP draw on file
Data types labs anthropometrics

SYS-P4

ATM heterozygosity is unrelated to the metabolic picture and needs its own track

high n/a

A pathogenic ATM variant is on file. It carries surveillance and cascade-testing obligations that do not interact with any of the three metabolic patterns above and must not be traded off against them.

Mechanism
Loss of one functional copy of ATM reduces double-strand break repair capacity. The consequence is a lifetime cancer risk modifier and a radiosensitivity consideration, neither of which is affected by glycemic or hemodynamic control.
Signals
  • · ATM heterozygous P/LP, reported on the 160-gene panel
  • · no first-degree relative testing recorded
  • · family history T2D and HTN, no premature cancer reported
Data types genetics family-history

Root-causes hypothesis

One process, two possible entry points. If sleep-disordered breathing is present, it is upstream of the insulin resistance, the blood pressure and part of the inflammation, and treating it first would move four domain tiers at once. If it is absent, the entry point is visceral adiposity and the order of operations is aerobic capacity and weight first, pharmacology second. The record cannot currently distinguish these two cases, and a home sleep apnea test is the cheapest thing that would.

Open questions

  • ·Is the overnight resting heart rate of 38 bpm explained by conditioning, by medication, or by high vagal tone between apneic events? VO2max of 28 mL/kg/min argues against conditioning.
  • ·Has infection been excluded before treating hs-CRP 3.0 mg/L as a chronic inflammatory signal?
  • ·Is the untreated hypertension sustained or clinic-only? No ambulatory series is on file.
  • ·Was the ATM result returned with a genetic counseling encounter, and were first-degree relatives informed?

Absent but expected

  • ·Sleep study of any kind, given a documented SpO2 nadir of 89 %.
  • ·Ambulatory blood pressure monitoring in a patient carrying an untreated stage 2 reading.
  • ·Colorectal cancer screening, due at 45 and never performed.
  • ·Liver imaging or a fibrosis score, given HOMA-IR 4.3 with a waist of 105 cm.
  • ·Any repeat of hs-CRP, ApoB or Lp(a). Every value on file is a single draw.

Noise excluded

  • ·eGFR 93 mL/min/1.73m² with UACR 14 mg/g: within range, and the small UACR elevation is accounted for by the blood pressure rather than by a separate renal process.
  • ·Mood 6 of 10 and body ease 5 of 10: not separable from the energy and clarity scores at this sample size, so no independent affective pattern is asserted.
  • ·Single low overnight heart-rate readings on 3 of 14 nights: within the coverage error of an 82 %-complete wearable series.
  • ·Family history of hypertension: already carried by the group D term, not counted twice here.
