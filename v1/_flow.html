Version 1

The canonical reference surface, recreated

v1 is not a proposal. It is the ratified reference surface (aleron-actionmap-al47m-ds.html) rebuilt against the physician register and the canonical component set, so that the thing the team already agreed to can be compared, screen for screen, against what ships today. Where the reference surface violates a law it ratified later, this recreation follows the law and the divergence is annotated on the screen.

One patient throughout: AL-47M, Ethan Park, 47, metabolic-risk phenotype. The reference surface is a single-page chart with a six-section rail and no route above it, so there is no navigation to draw: the flow below is what a physician can reach from where, not a sequence they walk through.

Where a chart is entered

Entry Deep link into one patient’s chart aleron.md/chart/AL-47M/…

There is no step before this one. The reference surface has no login and no patient list: the chart is opened by URL, already scoped to a patient, and the patient switcher in the rail is a select element over a fixture array. Everything a physician does before reaching a chart is what v2 has to build.

The rail · six sections, fixed per surface family

1 Patient Data patient-data.html 2 Risk risk-models.html 3 Vitality vitality.html 4 Care Plan care-plan.html 5 Journal journal.html 6 Aleron AI aleron-ai.html

The rail is identical on all six screens and its order never changes. Register O4 fixes rails per surface family, which is why the rail is a component with a declared item set rather than a free-form nav.

Risk splits in two

2a · Models tab Five disease models, one selected domain risk-models.html 2b · Actions tab The action map and the evidence ledger risk-actions.html

Two tabs under one heading, not two rail sections. The split is the argument: a model output is a description, and the action map is what that description is worth. Vitality carries the same Models / Actions switch, but its Actions pane plots the same levers on a second, non-QALY scale and its own caption warns that it is not a new score. That pane is left out of this recreation and flagged on the Vitality screen.

Cross-surface handoffs

Three, and only three. The reference surface is deliberately shy about linking between sections: the rail is the navigation, and a link inside a screen has to be earned by the physician actually needing to be somewhere else at that moment.

From Risk Continue in Aleron AI risk-actions.html
→ Lands on Aleron AI, patient context already loaded aleron-ai.html
From Care Plan Open Action Space care-plan.html
→ Lands on The action map that priced the draft risk-actions.html
From Care Plan View as the journal entry it will become care-plan.html
→ Lands on Journal, the draft selected on the spine journal.html

The Care Plan pair is the doctrine in two links. A care plan is a note before its signature, so it points backwards at the map that priced it and forwards at the entry it is about to become. Nothing points at a release step, because there is no release step to point at.

Where v1 stops

These are not omissions from the recreation. They are absent from the reference surface itself, and they are the reason v2 exists.

  • No queue. Nothing tells a physician which chart to open next. There is no inbox, no panel, no review list and no unread state anywhere on the surface. A physician who does not already know the patient’s code cannot get in.
  • No login. No authentication, no session, no role. The surface has one implied user, Dr. A. Okafor, and no way to be anyone else, which also means no way to model who is allowed to sign, prescribe or release.
  • No ordering UI. Orders appear as rows with statuses, and both places a physician would act on one are inert: Sign & release ships permanently disabled, and there is no lab-order screen, no standing-order screen, and no authorization step. The surface can describe an order and cannot place one.
  • No patient list and no cohort. The rail’s patient switcher is a select element over two fixtures. Everything a practice needs in order to run, rather than to demonstrate, sits outside this surface.

v0 has all four of those and none of the instrument. v1 has the instrument and none of those. v2 is the argument that the second set is easier to add to the first than the reverse.
