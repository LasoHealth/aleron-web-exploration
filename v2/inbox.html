Where these states come from

Every word in the lifecycle column is a state in the Role and Release Matrix, section 4, not a label invented for this screen. The queue shows only states whose Physician visible column is not None, and the Next act button is the action that state permits under section 3.

Results are not a lifecycle state, which is why they are not a tenth word in that column. A result lands on a patient wherever they already stand, and the two lists split on how the result reached you: a critical value is sent, an abnormal one is found. The abnormal list carries the time of the sweep that built it, because a list nobody was told about is only as complete as the last time somebody looked.

What changed, and where it came from

new Result review, and why it is two surfaces rather than one. Under the delegated lab flow, validating a result is this physician's job, and nothing on this screen carried that obligation. The vendor emits labtest.result.critical and no labtest.result.abnormal, and a partial result fires no webhook at all, the stated reason being "the possibility of critical values". Canvas adds nothing: AuditEvent and Subscription both return 404 on the live aleronmd-dev instance, so there is no push mechanism of any kind on that side. Abnormals therefore have to be polled, from GET /v3/orders filtered on is_critical and interpretation. Three shapes were on the table and only one survives the mechanism.
Rejected, a tenth lifecycle state. A result is not a position in the lifecycle. It lands on a patient wherever they already stand, so Mara Chen would have to leave Plan editing to acknowledge a lab and then find her way back into it. It would also make the rank table lie: rank is "how far along is this plan", and an abnormal potassium is not further along than anything.
Rejected, one Results tab holding both. That is the shape the brief warns about. A critical value is sent to you within minutes; an abnormal value is here because a sweep ran, and the newest one may be twenty minutes older than the sweep. Merging them puts a guaranteed delivery and a best-effort discovery in one column under one heading, and the physician cannot tell which they are scanning.
Built, a standing region plus a tab. Critical sits above the counts strip with no data-queues attribute at all, so it is structurally outside the filter and no tab can hide it, which is the drawn form of "pushed". Abnormal is the third tab, headed with the sweep time, which is the drawn form of "polled". Different column head as well: Delivered against Found. Reading it aloud is the test, and the two sentences do not sound alike.

finding The rail badge still reads 7 and result review is not in it. That is not an oversight, it is the mechanism showing through. The badge is GET /Task?owner=Practitioner/{id}&status=requested&_count=1 read off Bundle.total, and a result is not a Task: it lives in the lab vendor and reaches Aleron by poll. Making the badge 11 would mean either minting a Task per result, which puts a lab value's existence into the shared chart before a physician has looked at it, or summing two systems client-side and calling it one count. Both are decisions above this screen. So the badge counts Task-backed work, the tab badges count what is in the tab, and the discrepancy is left visible rather than papered over.

finding The queue primitive is Canvas Task, and this is where that gets said. GET /Task?owner=Practitioner/{id}&status=requested&_count=100 is a genuine cross-patient query, one of only two on this screen, and a Task already carries every field the row needs: for is the patient, owner is the physician, authoredOn is the Waiting column, note is the What is waiting sentence, and input labels are free text, auto-created and searchable with label=, so the lifecycle word is the query key. The patient column is the second call, GET /Patient?_has:CareTeam:participant:member=Practitioner/{id}. That is what lets the queue live in Canvas, so no patient identifier ever has to reach Aleron to build it, and every count here is Bundle.total on a _count=1 search, so not one of the four tiles fetches the rows it counts.
Two costs come with it. Labels have no enum and no validation, so a typo does not fail, it silently creates a tenth state and LIFECYCLE_RANK drops that row to 99. And on the live instance Task is granted at system scope and not at user scope, so a physician-scoped token may not reach it at all. That is unresolved and it is load bearing: if it holds, this queue reads under a service token while every write in the ordering flow reads under the physician's, and the two do not see the same thing.

finding There is no single priority field to sort on, and that is deliberate. The question was whether the care plan or the profile carries one. Nothing does. Panel states the position outright: neither list ranks patients by the action layer's own output: the lifecycle is the sort key, because a packet that is ready outranks one that is merely alarming. What exists per patient is lifecycle state, an ordinal from Role and Release Matrix v1 section 4, and wait duration. Both are already the sort. The action map's QALY figures price actions within one patient, not patients against each other, and the six domain risks are six values of which five carry model provenance pending, so ranking on them would be ranking on unvalidated models. The Priority routine seen on an order is per order, not per patient.

finding A custom order has nowhere to persist, which is why the demo does not pretend to. Aleron stores no patient data and Canvas has no per-physician list order: the nearest field is Task.priority, which accepts only stat, urgent and routine, three buckets rather than a total order, so it cannot express "Mara above Helena". Persisting one would need a Canvas Custom Data Model keyed by physician, and that is a product decision about whether a physician's private triage order is patient data. Until it is taken, a custom order is a per-viewer view preference and belongs in the browser, not the chart.
