How the four outcomes are combined
Composite
None emitted. There is no Vitality score. Four outcomes are four numbers, and averaging them would let a good mood hide low energy.
Comparison
Within-person only. No percentile, no cohort, no normative band beyond the desired range shown on each chart.
Device role
Corroboration. Device instruments corroborate recovery and sleep; they do not replace patient-reported outcomes or determine protocol state.
Cadence
Repeat the four measures at 30, 90 and 180 days. Conditional instruments, PEG-3 and PHQ or GAD, fire only if the outcome pattern calls for them.

What changed, and where it came from

fixed There was no way to log out, and no way to tell who was logged in. .prail__patient names the patient, so across 21 screens nothing said whose session this was and nothing ended it. v0 has both in its header, Dr. A. Okafor beside a Log out button, and the proposals dropped them when the header became a rail. A clinical surface that cannot say who is signed in cannot be handed to the next physician at the same workstation, which is the ordinary case in a clinic. The rail foot now carries the actor and the way out, under a rule, at the bottom where the least frequent act belongs. Proposed in section B as a domain.chart-rail addition, using the foot's existing exemption for a non-section action rather than claiming a new one.

fixed As bare underlined text the control was 15px tall, for the one act that ends the session. A text link is the sanctioned shape where a filled control will not fit, per register O5's fallback, but the hit area still has to be real: the label keeps its size and the target grew to 32px, with the same pointer: coarse guard the row-density variant uses restoring the full 44px floor on touch. Measured 14.35:1 flight-deck and 5.34:1 day.

new Vitality is now a half of this screen rather than a rail stop of its own. Patient Data is what is measured, Vitality is what is felt, and the HRV block is the hinge the reference surface already calls the bridge: its triptych reads subjective outcome measures, HRV recovery context, physiologic driver interpretation, which names both halves in the reference surface's own words. Reading the felt report used to cost a rail hop away from the labs it has to be read against, and it no longer does. The rail returns to the six sections that register O4 fixes for this surface family, so the chart stops carrying a seventh item the icon set never had a glyph for.

new The screen itself, carried forward from v1/patient-data.html. v0 has no patient-data view at all: its EMR tab is a deep link that hands the physician to Canvas. This is the surface that keeps the scan in Aleron.

new The felt half, from the reference surface's Vitality pane: four outcome charts on one fixed time scale, the HRV context block with its three-part bridge caption, and the associated drivers explorer. The later app build removed the Vitality tab from navigation as out of scope for V1, leaving vitalityView() and its adapter path in place but unrouted, so the section became unreachable rather than deleted. v2 restores the content and gives it the one home where it is read against the labs.

new The fixed x-scale runs from −28d to 180d whether or not a value exists at each point, and the three future windows are ghost dots on the axis rather than a projected line. A projection would be a decorative trend on one measured timepoint.

new The gold wash is the desired-range band, which P6 names explicitly as tokened chart furniture alongside gridlines and reference bands. The personal-baseline band on the HRV chart and the expected-range band on the driver chart are ink washes instead, because neither is a target the plan is aiming at.

new Low bands are filled ember tiles, per P3, and Low vs baseline on HRV is an advisory word rather than a tile, because a recovery signal running under its own baseline is context and not a hazard.

carried The Device instruments group, from the later app build's wearableSummary.js and dashboardApp.js. Five admitted instruments, each with a sparkline, a trend_state word and its window density. v1 had one sparkline on one row and no vocabulary for how much history stood behind it.

carried The coverage line and the instrument boundary sentence, both verbatim from dashboardApp.js. The boundary is the reason the group is called device instruments rather than outcomes: it corroborates, it does not score.

new Group headers are the only tracked caps on the surface, per P5's scan clause. Everything that reads as a sentence stays sentence case.

gap The rail is PROPOSED domain.chart-rail in physician-chrome.css. components.css v0.5.0 declares 28 components and none of them is a navigation rail, yet the register fixes its width, icon size and selection treatment. Ratify or replace.

gap Screening has no canonical icon. The magnifier with a plus is drawn inline in the canonical stroke grammar, matching the glyph the later app build uses, and is flagged provisional in the markup. Reusing risk for it would collapse two different sections onto one shape.

gap Nothing in components.css covers a PRO card, a fixed-scale outcome chart, a bridge caption or a driver explorer. All four are screen-local layout here; if a second surface needs the outcome card it graduates to section B of physician-chrome.css.

dropped v0's .ds-skeleton loading state, which this group would have shown while the wearable window resolved. The canonical loading instrument is three pulsing dots (.amd-loading); shimmer is on BRIEF.md's forbidden list and the v0 use is a tracked finding, not a pattern to carry.

dropped Nothing from v0 is carried into the felt half, because v0 has nothing to carry: the current portal models five disease domains plus a systemic pass and records no patient-reported outcome anywhere. That absence is the finding.

dropped The Models and Actions tab pair that sat in the Vitality header. It is the risk surface's own view switch, borrowed there because Vitality was a rail stop beside it; on a patient-data screen it would offer a view this screen does not have.
