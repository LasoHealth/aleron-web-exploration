What changed, and where it came from

fixed There was no way to log out, and no way to tell who was logged in. .prail__patient names the patient, so across 21 screens nothing said whose session this was and nothing ended it. v0 has both in its header, Dr. A. Okafor beside a Log out button, and the proposals dropped them when the header became a rail. A clinical surface that cannot say who is signed in cannot be handed to the next physician at the same workstation, which is the ordinary case in a clinic. The rail foot now carries the actor and the way out, under a rule, at the bottom where the least frequent act belongs. Proposed in section B as a domain.chart-rail addition, using the foot's existing exemption for a non-section action rather than claiming a new one.

fixed As bare underlined text the control was 15px tall, for the one act that ends the session. A text link is the sanctioned shape where a filled control will not fit, per register O5's fallback, but the hit area still has to be real: the label keeps its size and the target grew to 32px, with the same pointer: coarse guard the row-density variant uses restoring the full 44px floor on touch. Measured 14.35:1 flight-deck and 5.34:1 day.

new The six-cell context strip, the conversation log with its fixed provenance footer, the composer and the four canned prompts are the reference surface's Aleron AI pane, carried unchanged. v0 has no AI surface of any kind.

carried Claim adoption, from the later app build's aiColleagueView.js: the clinical thought card with its type and state, the confidence line with its main limitation, the four evidence sections, the confidence conditions and provenance disclosure, and the two-step confirmation with its blockquote and boundary sentence, all verbatim in structure. The reference surface's agent produces prose and stops there, so a physician who agrees with it has nowhere to put the agreement.

new Contradictory evidence and missing information sit beside support, at equal width, above the controls. An adoption control that is reachable before the contradictions have been read is the interface arguing for its own claim.

new Adopt is a two-step act and the second step restates the exact claim in a blockquote. The quoted text is what is adopted, not the conversation around it, and the boundary sentence says what the act does and does not do.

new .amd-confidence is canonical and is used as declared, moderate at three of five bars. The band word beside it is a baseline word rather than a tile, because low confidence is not a hazard; it is the reason to consult before adopting.

gap The thought card has no canonical component. .amd-proposed-action is the nearest and it is a different object: a proposed act with accept and reject controls, not a claim with evidence for and against it. The card is screen-local here and is a strong candidate for section B once Care Plan's proposal card is settled, since the two share the adopt-then-confirm shape.

gap .amd-button's 44 px floor is again wrong for a card-level control row; the local override shrinks it to 32 px. Same finding as the action-map ledger: the registry needs a dense variant.

gap Screening has no canonical icon. The magnifier with a plus is drawn inline in the canonical stroke grammar and flagged provisional in the markup.

dropped The later app build's full consultation menu, six consultation types plus a specialist lens select, rendered inline on every expanded card. It is collapsed to one Consult control here: six buttons of equal weight beside Adopt and Dismiss makes the two acts that matter the same size as four that do not.
