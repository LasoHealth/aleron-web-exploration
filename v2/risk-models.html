Patient picture

A 47-year-old man whose central adiposity sits upstream of almost everything else in the packet. Blood pressure, glycemia, triglycerides, inflammation and low fitness are all consistent with one visceral-adiposity story, and two findings do not fit it: an overnight oxygen nadir of 89 % and an overnight heart rate of 38 bpm. Those two are the reason the plan starts with a sleep and rhythm question rather than with a prescription.

P1

Sleep-disordered breathing as an upstream driver of blood pressure, insulin resistance and fatigue

medium confidence snapshot

Four findings that three different engines each own a piece of are consistent with one untreated upstream driver. No engine tests for it, because none of them has a sleep input.

Intermittent nocturnal hypoxia drives sympathetic surge and cortisol, which raises nocturnal and morning blood pressure and worsens hepatic insulin resistance; unrefreshing sleep then presents as low energy and low clarity.

Root-causes hypothesis, open questions, and what was excluded
Root causes
One hypothesis explains most of the picture: untreated sleep-disordered breathing on a background of visceral adiposity. Weight, blood pressure, glycemia and fatigue read as downstream of those two, which is why the map prices the sleep gate ahead of the antihypertensive.
Open questions
Is the nocturnal bradycardia a consequence of sleep-disordered breathing or an independent conduction finding? Does alcohol intake account for any of the triglyceride and hepatic pattern? Is the hs-CRP of 3.0 mg/L chronic, or a single acute-phase draw?
Absent but expected
Ambulatory night blood-pressure series · FIB-4 · alcohol intake in drinks per day · 12-lead ECG · a second hs-CRP
Noise excluded
eGFR 93 mL/min/1.73m² and UACR 14 mg/g are both normal and were considered and dropped. Lp(a) 25 nmol/L is normal and carries no overlay here. Never-smoker status removes the lung engine from the cancer question. None of these four is part of any pattern above.

What changed, and where it came from

fixed There was no way to log out, and no way to tell who was logged in. .prail__patient names the patient, so across 21 screens nothing said whose session this was and nothing ended it. v0 has both in its header, Dr. A. Okafor beside a Log out button, and the proposals dropped them when the header became a rail. A clinical surface that cannot say who is signed in cannot be handed to the next physician at the same workstation, which is the ordinary case in a clinic. The rail foot now carries the actor and the way out, under a rule, at the bottom where the least frequent act belongs. Proposed in section B as a domain.chart-rail addition, using the foot's existing exemption for a non-section action rather than claiming a new one.

fixed As bare underlined text the control was 15px tall, for the one act that ends the session. A text link is the sanctioned shape where a filled control will not fit, per register O5's fallback, but the hit area still has to be real: the label keeps its size and the target grew to 32px, with the same pointer: coarse guard the row-density variant uses restoring the full 44px floor on touch. Measured 14.35:1 flight-deck and 5.34:1 day.
